PROPORTIONAL SYMBOL MAPS
Proportional symbol maps are a good choice 
when points or areas carry a quantitative 
attribute. 
The map on the right uses length-propor-
tional bars to show Covid-19 cases, where 
the attribute belongs to an area geometry.  
The map below uses area-proportional cir-
cles to show the size of craters, where the 
attribute belongs to a point geometry. Size is 
the channel used in both cases. In the ﬁrst 
map the marks are triangles, though lines 
are more common; in the second they are 
circles, though other shapes such as area-
proportional squares are equally possible. 
Proportional symbol maps can show a few 
dozen items. Beyond that, the symbols heav-
ily overplot and the map becomes difﬁcult to 
read.

Other symbol types are possible, such as 
stacked bar charts or pie charts, as on the 
map showing the percentage of alternative 
energy customers in Germany a few years 
ago. 
DESIGN PRINCIPLES 
Overlapping symbols are possible. The read-
ability of overlapping symbols can be in-
creased by applying strokes to symbols (cen-
tre) or making symbols (partially) transparent 
(right). 
 
Colour can be used for double encoding of 
the same attribute, or to show a second 
attribute (as shown by the hydrology map 
on the previous page). 
 
The size of symbols requires consideration. 
Symbols that are too small make values hard 
to compare; symbols that are too large 
overplot, hiding the geographic position of 
the symbols and making comparison of val-
ues difﬁcult.
